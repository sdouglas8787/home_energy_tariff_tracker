Always on

"Always on" (baseload)

At your quietest hour (around 01:00, asleep, nothing in use) you still draw about 0.33 kWh every hour. That constant background load is roughly 2,861 kWh a year (~£670/yr), about 69% of your electricity. That is unusually high (a typical home is 20 to 40%), so something is very likely drawing power around the clock without you realising.

Likely suspects at that level: a second or old fridge/freezer, an aquarium or wine fridge, electric hot-water recirculation or an immersion left on, underfloor heating or a dehumidifier, or a stack of always-on electronics. To find it, switch things off one at a time and watch whether the overnight level drops.

Method

Usage basis: recency-weighted seasonal, bias-corrected (walk-forward). The forecast is a recency-weighted seasonal model (recent months weighted more), bias-corrected by a walk-forward test — at each past month the model is rebuilt on only-earlier data, predicted, and compared to the actual, and the average error is used to calibrate the forecast. The 95% band reflects that test's error spread. Gas volumes are converted to kWh; a supplier's calorific value varies, so figures are a close estimate, not a bill.
