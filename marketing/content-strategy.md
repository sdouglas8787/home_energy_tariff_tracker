# Content strategy and calendar

One project becomes one evergreen article, which becomes weeks of LinkedIn, all pointing back
to a single hub. This file is the plan. Everything here is em-dash-free so it pastes clean.

## The confidentiality rule (read first)

Nothing from an employer goes out. No employer data, code, folders, screenshots, client names,
or internal metrics, ever, on Medium, LinkedIn, GitHub, or the hub. Employer work can only
appear as a fully anonymised case study, and only with written permission. When in doubt,
leave it out. The energy project is safe because it is entirely your own and uses your own
(anonymised) data.

Tag every project before it goes near the hub:
- PUBLIC: your own work, safe to show in full (the energy engine).
- CASE STUDY: employer or client work, only ever anonymised, only with permission.
- PRIVATE: never leaves your machine.

## The model

Build project, then write one Medium article (the evergreen reference), then publish, then
share on LinkedIn, then mine 4 to 6 LinkedIn posts out of that article, then polish the GitHub
repo, then repeat with the next project. LinkedIn drives traffic, Medium holds the depth, the
hub ties it together.

## Cadence (sustainable alongside a job)

- One Medium article every 4 to 6 weeks.
- Two LinkedIn posts per week.
- Quality over frequency, always. A month of silence beats a weak post.

## The article series (your recognisable body of work)

Each future article expands one idea from the anchor piece, or a new project phase. Give each a
lead image from the dashboard.

1. I nearly made a £421 mistake on my energy tariff. (DONE, the anchor.)
   Lead image: the what-if screenshot.
2. Why I don't trust a forecast without back-testing.
   Angle: walk-forward validation and bias correction, explained for decision-makers, not just
   modellers. The point: a forecast without an error bar is a confident guess.
   Lead image: usage and forecast chart with the confidence band.
3. How the weather hid a rise in my energy use.
   Angle: weather normalisation and heating-degree-days. A flat bill can hide a real change.
   Lead image: the year-on-year heat-map or the weather overlay.
4. Monte Carlo isn't just for finance.
   Angle: stop asking which option is cheapest, ask the probability it is cheaper, and size the
   downside. Uncertainty as a product feature.
   Lead image: the Monte Carlo range chart.
5. What half-hourly smart-meter data changes. (AFTER the data connects.)
   Angle: from monthly averages to a load profile, and why time-of-use offers only make sense
   at that resolution. Your dishwasher-after-dinner example.
   Lead image: an hour-by-day load profile (to be built).
6. Building a decision engine instead of a dashboard.
   Angle: the design philosophy. A dashboard that answers a question beats one that displays
   data. The strongest personal-brand piece.
   Lead image: the full four-tab dashboard.

By article 3 or 4, readers start associating your name with practical analytics, not one-off
projects.

## LinkedIn playbook

- Tell the story, do not announce an article. Lead with the surprise or the decision, then the
  insight, then a reason to click.
- Put the Medium link in the first comment, not the post body. LinkedIn suppresses reach on
  posts with outbound links in the body.
- Post manually, not via Buffer or Zapier. The value is the first 30 to 60 minutes of replying
  to comments and joining the discussion, which auto-posting cannot do.
- One clear image per post. Crop to the single card you are describing.
- Consistent theme across screenshots (light for articles, or dark for LinkedIn punch, pick one).

Example story-first opener:
"I nearly switched to an energy tariff that would have cost me £421 more a year. Every
comparison site recommended it. They all reached the same conclusion because they all made the
same assumption: that households with similar annual usage behave similarly. Mine doesn't. That
sent me down a rabbit hole that ended in a forecasting engine with back-testing, weather
normalisation and Monte Carlo simulation. The article explains what I built and, more
importantly, why."

## GitHub repo checklist (they read the README, not the code)

- README with screenshots up top (the dashboard, not code).
- A short "what it does and why" opening, before any setup instructions.
- Example dashboard (the DEMO html once we anonymise it).
- Sample Excel workbook (the DEMO input).
- A simple architecture or data-flow diagram.
- Forecast methodology section (recency-weighting, walk-forward, bias correction, in plain terms).
- Roadmap.
- Example outputs / a link to the Medium article.
- No real data, no meter identifiers, no employer material.

## Medium publications to target

Submit the article to established publications, not just your own profile, for far more reach.
Look at ones covering: Data Science, Python, Analytics, Forecasting, Energy, Business
Intelligence. Search Medium for "data science" and "analytics" publications open to submissions,
read their guidelines, and pitch the anchor article. If accepted you reach their audience, not
only your followers.
